Part 1: Universal core (every role)
1. Tell me about yourself
They are testing: Whether you can summarise a career in under 90 seconds and link it to this role.
How to answer: Present role, then one proof point with a number, then why it fits this job. Stop there and let them
ask.
Likely follow-ups: Why are you leaving / looking? / What are you proudest of?
2. Walk me through your highest-ownership project
They are testing: Real ownership versus participation. Every other question branches from this one.
How to answer: Problem, your exact role, the decisions you made, result, and what you would change. Choose
the project before the interview.
Likely follow-ups: Why did you choose this approach over another? / What went wrong? / How did you measure the
result?
3. Why this option and not that one?
They are testing: Whether you decided with reasons or just followed a default.
How to answer: Name the options, the criteria you used, what you chose, and the trade-off you knowingly
accepted.
Likely follow-ups: What would make you change that decision? / What did it cost you?

4. Tell me about a conflict with a stakeholder or teammate
They are testing: Whether you find the real concern behind the disagreement.
How to answer: What each side wanted, what the underlying worry was, what you did, and the outcome. Keep it
factual and avoid blaming.
Likely follow-ups: What if they outranked you? / What would you do differently?
5. Tell me about a failure or a mistake
They are testing: Self-awareness and whether you change your process after a mistake.
How to answer: A real, specific example, what you owned, and the process change you made afterwards.
Likely follow-ups: How did you tell stakeholders? / Has it happened again?
6. The scenario just got harder: what do you do?
They are testing: Whether you escalate honestly or just promise to push through.
How to answer: Triage by priority, check what can flex (scope, date, quality checks), then raise the real trade-off
early with options.
Likely follow-ups: What if they refuse the trade-off? / What do you drop first?
7. Why this company and why this role?
They are testing: Whether you did homework and whether the fit is real.
How to answer: One specific fact about the company, one match to something you have actually done. Not
generic praise.
Likely follow-ups: Where do you see yourself in 3 years? / What would you do in your first 90 days?
8. Do you have any questions for us?
They are testing: Curiosity and whether you listened during the interview.
How to answer: Ask 2 or 3: what success looks like at 90 days, the biggest current challenge, and how the team
is structured around this role.

Part 2: Business Analyst module
R1. How do you gather requirements when stakeholders cannot say what they want?
They are testing: Elicitation method, not just 'I ask questions'.
How to answer: Interviews and workshops, observe the current process, map AS-IS, write requirements back
and get written confirmation, then prioritise (for example MoSCoW).
Your story to use: Mapped the Invoice-to-Collection and Reactivation workflows with BPMN (AS-IS and TO-BE)
and found 16 process gaps.
Likely follow-ups: What if two stakeholders conflict? / How do you know the requirements are complete?
R2. What is the difference between a BRD, FRS and user story? When do you use each?
They are testing: Documentation maturity.
How to answer: BRD: business goals and scope. FRS: system behaviour in detail. User story: one user-visible
need with acceptance criteria. Say which your team actually used and why.
Your story to use: Wrote 50 functional requirements, 23 non-functional requirements and 30 user stories with
acceptance criteria for the ERP/ISP platform.
Likely follow-ups: Write a user story with acceptance criteria for a renewal reminder. / How do you keep documents in
sync after changes?

R3. Walk me through an AS-IS and TO-BE process you mapped
They are testing: Process thinking and gap finding.
How to answer: Pick one process. Describe the AS-IS flow, where it broke (delays, manual steps, handoffs), the
TO-BE change and how you got sign-off.
Your story to use: Invoice-to-Collection and Reactivation workflows in Visio/BPMN with business rules and
decision points.
Likely follow-ups: Which tool did you use? / How did you find the gaps? / Who signed off?
R4. Functional versus non-functional requirements: give examples
They are testing: Basic BA vocabulary used correctly.
How to answer: Functional: what the system does. Non-functional: how well (performance, security, availability,
auditability). Give one example of each from your own project.
Your story to use: Your 23 non-functional requirements for the ERP platform.
Likely follow-ups: How do you test a non-functional requirement? / Which one gets missed most often?
R5. How do you prioritise when every stakeholder says theirs is urgent?
They are testing: Whether you use criteria or just yield to pressure.
How to answer: Agree criteria first (business value, risk, effort, dependency, deadline), score items together, and
make the trade-off visible to everyone.
Likely follow-ups: What if a senior stakeholder overrides the ranking? / Which framework do you prefer?
R6. A change request arrives in the middle of delivery. What do you do?
They are testing: Change control and impact analysis.
How to answer: Log it, assess impact on scope, time, cost and testing, get the right approver to decide, then
update requirements and traceability.
Likely follow-ups: Who approves it? / What if it breaks something already tested?
R7. How do you plan and run UAT?
They are testing: Whether you actually delivered a release.
How to answer: Entry criteria, test scenarios tied to requirements, who tests, defect severity versus priority, exit
criteria and sign-off.
Your story to use: Authored test plans and test cases yourself and coordinated SIT/UAT, defect analysis,
regression testing and release readiness.
Likely follow-ups: Severity versus priority: what is the difference? / UAT fails the day before go-live: what now?
R8. What is traceability and why does it matter?
They are testing: Discipline from requirement to test to release.
How to answer: Each requirement links to a design item and a test case so you can show coverage and see
what a change affects.
Likely follow-ups: How did you maintain it? / What did it catch?
R9. How do you validate data or check a report is correct?
They are testing: Practical SQL and reconciliation habits.
How to answer: Row counts, totals against a trusted source, duplicates, nulls, and spot checks on edge cases.
Say which SQL you actually write.
Your story to use: Reconciled payments to invoices and used validation rules and root-cause analysis for data
issues across CRM, billing and payment systems.
Likely follow-ups: How do you reconcile payments to invoices? / A number looks wrong in a report: first step?

R10. What is your Agile experience?
They are testing: Honest, specific ceremony experience.
How to answer: Name only the ceremonies you can back with an example, and describe your role in each

you do not hold.
Likely follow-ups: How do you write acceptance criteria? / Agile versus Waterfall: when would you choose each?
